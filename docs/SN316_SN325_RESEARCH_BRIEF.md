## What changed and why it matters

- **Second-level price data already got a fair try.** SN78 used 30-second index breakouts, bursts and fades with 1-second exit checks. The later untouched archive rejected those picks. SN135 and SN147 also rejected 1-second learners. The archives are index and opening-ATM CE/PE OHLC, so they support reconstruction of the price path. They cannot prove executable depth, live strike availability or lead/lag across the full chain. SN78's `ask_vec` modeled a spread. The newer Kite packets add best bids, asks, sizes and timestamps. That makes the proposed experiment a comparison of decision sequences at the same event and cost, with execution validated on packets. [SN78–SN79](research/fno/2026-11-13-sn78/README.md) · [SN135](research/fno/2026-11-13-sn131/README.md) · [SN147](research/fno/2026-11-13-sn141/README.md) · [Kite packet specification](https://kite.trade/docs/connect/v3/websocket/)
- **A faster clock adds both path information and noise.** The same 30-second bar can hide a clean hold or a break and recovery. A completed five-second bar can distinguish those before the next trade. Short-horizon price variation also reflects the spread, quote timing and intraday changes in liquidity. Derive all bars from the same feed, use completed bars only, match controls on time of day, and use the receipt clock for action and exchange timestamps for freshness. Spot helps locate a level. A valid futures midpoint is the traded underlying reference on packet data. Run identical controls and quotes so a feed change cannot masquerade as a better chart. [Aït-Sahalia and Mykland on sampling](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9611) · [Andersen and Bollerslev on intraday periodicity](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~boller/Published_Papers/joef_97.pdf)
- **Gold's latest results support leaving its settings alone.** The replay logged 86 losses, with 78 N11 exits and eight day stops. Adds contributed about ₹1.77 lakh net, and the top 10 trades supplied about 71% of net P&L. These are strong reasons to test whether an entry failure was observable early, while accounting for avoided losses *and* missed winners. The 0-of-62 scale-in overcommit result describes the replay. It does not remove the synthetic cash-check mismatch identified previously. True-cash sizing failed the frozen stress test, so leave it out of strategy selection. [SN310–SN312](research/fno/2026-11-16-sn301/README.md)
