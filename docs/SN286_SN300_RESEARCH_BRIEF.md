## Evidence that should change the next round

- **The partner's timing is the strongest clue to investigate.** SN121 found positive peaks on six of seven sessions and only three green closes. SN122 measured an option-mid move of +1.51% over the first minute versus +0.12% for random minutes in the same contract and day. At five minutes the entry comparison had p = 0.42. On the matched trips the partner's actual exit was better than both simple automated exits under the modeled costs. The original control sampled across the morning and afternoon and resampled trips individually. Seven sessions support a replication question. They give limited evidence about skill independent of clock, momentum, clustered re-entries and the use of minute snapshots. Review closes and fresh entries separately. The give-back message is already implemented. [SN121–SN130](research/fno/2026-11-13-sn121/README.md)
- **The size of that clue matters.** A 1.39 percentage-point difference in mid-price movement on an illustrative ₹6,000 one-lot debit is about ₹83 of gross improvement versus the random entry, before crossing, charges and delay. The useful question is whether executable returns at the intended latency are large enough to cover the bill. SN182's historical real-quote comparison estimated that about ₹214 per lot of the simulated friction came from the delay assumption. This supports measuring delay by setup, while retaining the ₹205 and ₹400 stresses. Research on US options shows execution timing can affect realized costs. Apply the measurement to this market. - **The old experiments leave a specific opening.** SN131 tested every minute and a separate opening-ATM one-second learner, so switching from 10:00 to many clock times is already an explored approach. SN136’s depth test used minute snapshots and six test days. The new recorder allows a narrower question about a sequence of future-book changes visible before the option actually reprices. Kite supplies top-five snapshot levels and timestamps. It does not identify individual orders or establish passive queue position. Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov's study is a rationale for measuring book imbalance. The Indian option's after-cost result is our experiment. - **WK-H options-only was a different payoff.** Static long wings cap the expiration loss of an intact, same-expiry iron fly, but they don't dynamically neutralize delta. SN251's ₹4,190 was the median modeled maximum loss for that fly in development. It is different from peak broker margin during entry, maintenance or exit. The tested flies and condors lost about ₹2.1–2.7 lakh per lot across the five samples. The ₹5 lakh WK-H figure is the system's unverified funding proxy for a paper unit. Leave WK-H out of the small-wallet work. ## Implementation contract for the next agent

1. **Run offline.** Start with SN286–SN290. Use sealed existing history for mechanisms and regression fixtures. The Oct 2020–Mar 2022 NIFTY and 2024–26 SENSEX buyer exams are now seen. Reserve Jan–Sep 2020 for the existing WK-H crash test. The history mentions a Jan–Apr 2026 one-second tranche separately. Verify its inventory and access history before claiming any untouched status. The planned main test obtains its new information from future five-level packets.
2. **Model real orders.** At each event save timestamps, selected strike/expiry/lot size, observed ask and bid depth, premium cost, exit clock, fill latency and fill/skip reason. Use a marketable-limit price bound, and count an order as missed if sufficient eligible depth never becomes observable at the bound in its fixed lifetime. A quote touch or best bid inferred after the fact cannot prove a fill. Track missing exits as unresolved and carry their risk through the ledger. Use all scheduled opportunities as the denominator.
3. **Enforce a recoverable loss.** Start at one lot. Distinguish premium plus fees at risk from a stop trigger, which can slip. A conservative candidate variant admits a trade only when the entire debit plus costs fits its allocated remaining risk, in addition to preserving Gold's cash. Test this openly, with ₹3,000 (10% of the starting wallet) as an existing research sub-budget reference inside the shared ₹6,000 day budget. Report disallowed trades so the owner sees what this restriction costs. Keep refills and growth identical to baseline. Incremental risk and direct contribution must be visible before any size change.
4. **Judge earning power at the required frequency.** At ten trades a month, ₹300 net per trade averages ₹150 per session in a 20-session month. Record incremental monthly cash flow, paid-from-profit days, shortfalls, worst day/month, drawdown, refills, time capital is blocked and clustered losing runs. SN269's historical ₹500–750 payout depended on advances of ₹0.5–3.1 lakh. Evaluate any payout without outside advances as a separate paired cash-flow test with delayed or variable withdrawals.
