# Errata to rounds 5 and 5b (Nov 18, 2026, written before the first forward-cohort session)

The independent audit of `9471abfd` (`docs/research/simulation/2026-11-18-expiry-review-en56/AUDIT.md`) found these. I checked each against the code and the data, and agree with all of them.
- Nothing scored is rewritten. `results5.json`, `results5b.json`, their start receipts and freezes stay as they are.
- `FORWARD5.md` keeps its original text in git (`8b347766`).

## A. EN55's forward test: calendar and qualification

### A1. Calendar correction

`FORWARD5.md` named Nov 20 (SENSEX) and Nov 24 (NIFTY) as the first sessions. Both are wrong. The dated recorder catalogue of Nov 17 (`tr01-export-20261118/2026-11-17/instruments.jsonl`) lists these expiring contracts:

| Index | Expiries in the catalogue |
|---|---|
| SENSEX (BFO) | 2026-11-19, 2026-11-26, 2026-12-03 |
| NIFTY (NFO) | 2026-11-23, 2026-12-01 |

- Nov 24 is an NSE F&O holiday, so NIFTY's November expiry is **Monday Nov 23**.
- The first cohort sessions are therefore **SENSEX Nov 19** and **NIFTY Nov 23**.

**The rule is unchanged:** the cohort is the first 20 *actual* expiry sessions per index after Nov 18, 2026.
- A session is identified by the expiry date of the expiring contract in a dated instrument catalogue (the recorder's, or the broker's instrument master for that day). Weekday arithmetic is never used.
- No session is skipped or invented.

### A2. Data for each session (fixed precedence, decided now)

1. **Breeze 1-minute candles** (download skill), when the index, the front future and the expiring chain all cover 12:00–15:29.
2. **Otherwise, a recorder export** converted into minute bars by a builder that has passed a parity check against Breeze candles on a seen day. That builder is round 6's EN70, and each export needs the owner's OK.
3. **Otherwise the session is UNAVAILABLE.** It is reported as missing and still counts among the 20; it is never replaced by a later session.

### A3. The comparison stays; it is now labelled a comparison

`FORWARD5.md`'s four conditions are kept word for word: wallet, fixed-cap ordinary and adverse, net without the best expiry, and the worst expiry, each on each index against E03.2.

The result after 20 sessions per index is reported as **"COMPARISON: EN55 ahead"** or **"COMPARISON: EN55 not ahead"**.
- It is a paper comparison, not a qualification.
- An arm that loses less than E03.2, or trades almost never, can be "ahead" here. That is the audit's point, and the label says so.

### A4. A separate absolute qualification layer, registered now

**Support facts (seen development books, `power_erratum.py` writing `power-erratum.json`):**

| | NIFTY | SENSEX |
|---|---:|---:|
| EN55 funded parents per expiry | 0.345 | 0.159 |
| Parents expected in 20 sessions | about 7 | about 3 |
| Standard deviation of EN55 net per expiry | Rs 799 | Rs 657 |
| Standard deviation of EN55 − E03.2 per expiry | Rs 215 | Rs 149 |
| Expiries needed to detect Rs 50 a session over E03.2 (one-sided 5%, 80% power) | 114 | 56 |
| Expiries needed to detect Rs 50 a session of absolute profit | about 1,580 | about 1,070 |

**The qualification cohort:** the first **40** actual expiries per index after Nov 18, 2026. This is about 40 weeks and about 20 EN55 parents across both indices together. It is fixed now and is neither extended nor shortened.

**EN55 is QUALIFIED FOR A PAPER-MAIN REVIEW only if all of these hold on the combined two-index ledger of that cohort:**
1. at least 20 funded EN55 parents;
2. fixed-cap net > 0 at ordinary **and** at adverse fills;
3. net without its single best expiry ≥ 0;
4. neither index's Rs 30,000 wallet reaches the Rs 4,000 halt;
5. the four `FORWARD5.md` comparisons against E03.2 hold on the combined ledger.

**Reported, but not a condition:**
- intratrade liquidation drawdown (the worst marked equity inside a session, not only at session close);
- a combined chronological Rs 30,000 wallet across both indices, with shared reservations.

**The verdicts:**
- **Fewer than 20 funded parents:** INCONCLUSIVE. A continuation needs a new written registration before its first session, and both reports are published.
- **Enough parents but a condition fails:** NOT QUALIFIED.

**What this cannot do:** the table shows that 40 weeks cannot prove a steady absolute edge of Rs 50 a session; that would take more than a thousand expiries. A pass would mean the result is consistent with a profitable, controlled book and earns a paper review. It is never real-money qualification, and it never increases size.

## B. Round 5b's resting-bid measurement (Q28): labels corrected

**1. The two fill rules are different proxies, not bounds.**
- The "conservative" rule counts the whole increase in cumulative day volume whenever the latest traded price is at or below our bid. A snapshot does not say at what prices that volume traded, so it can count volume that traded above our bid.
- The "optimistic" rule needs a later ask at or below our bid, or a trade 5 paise below it.
- Neither contains the other. At 60 seconds the saved rates are 88.75% ("conservative") against 88.33% ("optimistic").

**The relabels:**

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| conservative fill | volume-at-bid proxy |
| optimistic fill | price-through proxy |
| The minute-candle rule agreed 97.5% with packet evidence | The minute-candle rule agreed with the volume-at-bid proxy on 97.5% of orders on one session |

This is agreement between two models, not accuracy against exchange fills. The recorder's feed (Kite "full" snapshots) cannot identify queue fills.

**2. The "adverse selection" numbers are a same-clock retrospective comparison.**
- The −2.1% / +17.6% figures are `(mid 60 s after the decision − our initial bid) / initial bid`, split by whether the volume proxy marked the order filled within those same 60 s.
- They are not returns after an actual fill.
- They do not use the decision midpoint: an unchanged mid of Rs 10.05 over a Rs 10 bid reads +0.5%.

They suggest that the orders a resting bid catches are the falling ones, which is still worth testing. They do not measure post-fill returns. Round 6 (EN58) measures decision-mid markouts, scenario-fill markouts and missed-winner cost separately.

## C. EN49's candidate list read the next bar

`rows49()` dropped a decision row when the *next* bar's buy would not fill under the limit. The live candidate universe therefore depended on future fills. The audit's counterexample (in its `verification.json`) changes only a later buy price, and the candidate list goes from two rows to zero.
- `rows49()` also forced one lot, while EN49's executor could buy up to three.
- **No trade was affected:** EN49 abstained everywhere.
- **The conclusion is narrowed.** The old reading was "a model trained on twelve thousand decisions never found a positive 3-minute trade". The new reading: "this six-feature, heavily shrunk ridge, with a one-lot 3-minute target and these execution assumptions, never forecast a positive trade".
- The highest saved forecasts were −4.08% (NIFTY) and −1.66% (SENSEX) of the reserve.
- Round 6 separates observable candidates from future labels and labels with the executed quantity (EN61).

## D. Wording

- **"At-the-money doesn't make 10x" (EN53).** The accurate statement: under that sizing and lifecycle, ATM tickets contributed less from the observed extreme winners. ATM options can multiply.
- **EN52's "kept leg trailed back to its cost".** That is a leg-level break-even, not a package break-even. The pair lost because the other leg had already been sold at a loss. Round 6's EN59 tests a whole-package exit.
- **EN55's halt margin.** Its worst ordinary session-close drawdown was Rs 3,613.14, Rs 386.86 below the halt. Intratrade marked drawdown was not reported; round 6 reports it.

## E. Round 5b harness gaps (no historical effect asserted)

- EN41's reversal exit checked that OFI was finite, but not the window-valid flag that entries required.
- `mid_at()` could return an arbitrarily old price.
- Execution helpers used receipt clocks without an exchange-age check on every packet used.

Round 6's packet code enforces validity, quote age and exchange-time freshness on every packet it reads.
