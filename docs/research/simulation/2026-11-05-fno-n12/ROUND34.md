Round 32 gave up large gains by capping the payoff. Test a distinct alternative that keeps the long-option upside while reallocating the one-lot budget. This first step measures whether the entry geometry is available often enough to justify a lifecycle. It makes no counterfactual cash settlement or profit estimate.

On a flat native N11T entry sized at one lot, inspect the first three 50-point strikes strictly out of the money in the same directional right and dated series. Require exact positive-volume option minutes at the native action clock, with reported volume at least the proposed 130 units, OI at least 5,000 and the modeled 1.5% spread limit. Resolve implied volatility and delta from the native observed forward and the archived option price with the existing pricing implementation. Use two whole lots only with absolute per-unit delta of at least 0.25 and total initial delta at least 110% of the one-lot native exposure. Rank passing strikes by largest total delta and then smaller full cash reserve. Each reserve includes full premium, costs to a zero exit, and the frozen native-style ₹205 or ₹210 adverse allowance for one or two lots. Require the two-lot reserve to fit within the original one-lot reserve.

Derive a premium stop using the native parent's estimated premium-backstop loss including fees and friction. Keep the tighter of that implied stop and the normal 25% candidate backstop. The entry bid must stand strictly above the result. Preserve original entry timestamps and never choose candidates using exit or later-day observations. Proceed to fixed-clock option pricing only with at least 20 qualifying entries across at least four of the five development windows. An entry price or model delta does not establish a fill or strategy return. Preserve the N012 materiality and risk gates in any later lifecycle test. No runtime restrictions are changed.
