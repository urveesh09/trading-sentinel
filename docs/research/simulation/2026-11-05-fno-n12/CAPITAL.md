# Capital study. Separate paper exposure from a one-lot trial

The owner supplied ₹250,000 paper and ₹15,000 for a future real-money trial. A trade with more capital committed is useful only if its added risk earns enough incremental P&L. For the paper account measure premium deployment on the sealed regular NIFTY dates. Include separately priced earned lots. Report peak, median active-day peak, time-weighted exposure, share of time holding exposure, and use of the nominal starting ₹37,500 premium cap. This is an exposure audit of the singles only. It excludes the separately tracked defined-risk book.

For the small account use a conditional one-lot mirror of the N11T parent trades. Compare the configured ₹12,500 and owner-specified ₹15,000 with the same existing ₹7,000 admission floor, one position at a time and three entries per day. Reserve a full lot at the native limit plus current round-trip zero-exit costs. Order earlier closures before new entries. Use the independent wallet's settled balance and the configured starting-capital premium cap. Reject unaffordable entries. Keep same strike and exit clock. Recompute the one-lot economics and ignore paper add-ons. All years start independently. The ₹7,000 floor is an inherited reference for comparability and would require a separate decision for a future ₹15,000 trial.

Standard uses the paper's modeled ask and exit bid. Adverse adds 1% to the ask before rounding and applying the native two-tick buffer and 2% chase cap, uses the limit as fill, cuts the exit bid by 1%, and charges another ₹205 per trade. These are fixed sensitivity assumptions. Same-clock modeled prices do not establish broker fills, quote freshness under routing, open-position stop parity, separate account kill switches or a five-session live authorization. Keep this labeled a conditional affordability diagnostic. Preserve decline counts, chronological cash, realized drawdown and each trade's price/cost identity. No broker operations or setting changes.
