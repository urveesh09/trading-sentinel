# Outside sources read for N11

Searched November 2, 2026. These informed the checks; no number in the study comes from them.

- **Volatility and intraday momentum.** Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum", *Journal of Financial Economics* 129(2), 2018: predictability is stronger on more volatile days. [Listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414), [author page](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/). It is on US data. Its index-futures results are read differently in later papers, so it supports the direction, not a size. This backs N11's size-by-volatility part, and partly the volatility half of the stand-aside.
- **Pausing on a strategy's own results.** I found no peer-reviewed or out-of-sample study. [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/equity-curve-based-throttling/) describes it as risk-shaping, not a source of edge, and says the signal lags. A [forum discussion](https://www.aussiestockforums.com/goto/post?id=886568) is sceptical: such a filter can keep you in when the market drops and out when the system would have gained. These are practitioner sources, so they are weak evidence, and their suggestion of testing for persistence and against simply trading smaller became check 14.
- **Adding to winners.** No peer-reviewed test of adding only when the stop has moved. [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/pyramiding.md) warns that adds in a range tend to fill near the top, and that exposure has to be capped. [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/position-sizing-in-trend-following-comparing-volatility-targeting-volatility-parity-and-pyramiding/) reports good in-house results on futures, with no out-of-sample detail. N11's add stays capped at one lot, and the random-entry control again shows it has no edge of its own.
