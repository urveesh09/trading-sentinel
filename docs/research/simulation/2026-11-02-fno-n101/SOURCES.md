# Mechanism references, not performance evidence

- Zerodha, [stop-loss order mechanics](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/charts-and-orders/order/articles/what-are-stop-loss-orders-and-how-to-use-them): triggering and executing a stop are distinct. N10.1 never fills at a requested protective stop merely because it was set; actual observed modeled bids determine exits, including adverse gaps.
- Zerodha, [policies and procedures](https://zerodha.com/policies-and-procedures/): risk-reducing orders may fail to execute during rapid moves. The study retains full-premium funding independently of the planned-stop calculation. No claim that the stop guarantees a profit or a maximum live loss.
- Zerodha, [option Greeks](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/glossary/futures-and-options/articles/what-are-option-greeks): time and implied volatility affect option premiums as well as the underlying move. This supports checking the option's own observed bid and price stop rather than treating an underlying trail as a guaranteed option break-even.
- Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu, [The Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf): selection among many backtests changes how a favorable historical result should be interpreted. N10.1 limits new hypotheses to two, keeps all old failures and calls every 2023–26 result seen-data research. It does not treat earlier PBO scores as a posterior probability that N10 works.
- Novy-Marx, [Backtesting Strategies Based on Multiple Signals](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21329): signal combination can magnify selection bias. No new entry predictor, side exclusion, market-day classifier or feature grid is introduced here.

Consulted November 2, 2026. These sources motivate conservative mechanics and evidence standards; none establishes profitability of this implementation. Trading performance comes only from the bound local replay artifacts.
